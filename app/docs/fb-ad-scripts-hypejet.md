# BlueFx Free Video Ad: Facebook/Instagram Scripts (Hype Jet 10 method)

Three finished 9:16 video ads for the free 40-second video ad offer. Each one can be made in the AI Media Machine with an AI presenter, inserted example clips and on-screen text.

## How these were built

HJ10 was used as the method. The parts that do the work in a 30-second direct response ad are:

- HJ10 Option 6, Acquire Clients: build the avatar, show the film, end with a price-qualified CTA. Option 3's rule also applies: hold the offer back until the payoff and put the CTA at the end only.
- Hook rules from the Hook and Hookz files: topic clarity plus on-target curiosity in the first 3 seconds. Avoid the four hook mistakes (delay, confusion, irrelevance, disinterest). Pick the key visual first. Make the visual, text, spoken and audio hooks say the same thing.
- The four horsemen: speed to value, contrast, comprehension clarity, curiosity loops. Plus atomic storytelling: cut every line that can go.
- The 13-step copy file: speak to someone, give a reason ("because"), own the flaw (the logo), show unique proof (Ascent), signal authority quietly (2008, 36,000+), use only real scarcity (one per business), make the CTA direct, write like you speak.

Timing rule used for every script: about 2.8 spoken words per second, which is a normal pace for an AI presenter. Each version is 81 to 84 spoken words, which comes to 29 to 30 seconds. Note: the current best version has about 109 spoken words. At a normal pace that runs about 38 seconds, not 27, so it would have to be read very fast or get cut. All three versions below are cut to fit.

## Production notes for all three

- Presenter: a plain AI presenter in business-casual clothes, speaking straight to camera in a normal office or workshop. No costume, eye patch or props. The presenter should look like someone a contractor or manufacturer would trust.
- Example clips: use only real BlueFx-made ads. Ascent Equipment is the hero clip. For quick cuts, use the contractor, laser clinic, farm and video digitizing shop ads. Do not use the realtor example.
- Captions: burn in large captions of the spoken words, because many people watch muted. Keep text out of the bottom third and the top strip, where Reels and Stories put their buttons.
- Honesty: every version shows "Free with a small BlueFx logo. $49 to remove it." on screen in the final scene, and the primary text says it too.
- Music: a light, steady business track at low volume under the voice. No sound effects except where a script says so.
- "Free" never appears in the first 3 seconds of any version. The live ad's "free first" opening is one reason it pulls weak leads.

## My pick: Version 1

Version 1 is the strongest replacement for the current best version. Here's why:

- It has the tightest hook. Eight words, under 3 seconds, and it names the condition the owner wants ("paying customers") without listing industries. The baseline hook ("Do you own a business with paying customers and a website, but want more customers?") is 15 words and takes about 5 seconds, which fails the Hookz file's delay and confusion tests (too slow, too clunky).
- It keeps every line the owner already approved (Facebook and Instagram "like you are right now", "so more of them call you", $1,000 or more, about 5 minutes, Ascent) and puts them in the plainest order: pain, where, result, proof, reason it's free, CTA.
- It's the only version that says the logo and $49 out loud. That's HJ10's price-qualified CTA, and it's what the three reviewers asked for. It should cut freebie seekers before the click, which matters more than clicks when only about 13% of leads buy.
- It fits 30 seconds at a normal speaking pace. The baseline doesn't.

If he wants a second ad to test later, Version 3 is the best challenger, because its hook is a different pain (calls that come and go), not a reworded version of the same one.

## Where I deviated from HJ10 to follow the owner's rules

- **Skipped the HJ10 session process.** I skipped the opening menu, the audio-mode question, style selection, the 12-panel storyboard, image generation and the "lock this?" loops. The brief already fixes the format (AI presenter voice-over, example clips, on-screen text), and the owner wants finished ads, not a process. I used Option 6 (Acquire Clients) with Option 3's "offer late, CTA at end" rule.
- **Dropped the cinematic engine.** I left out mystery in the first 2 seconds, "no exposition in the first 6 seconds", escalating stakes, a nightmare-level visible goal, the all-is-lost beat, twists and first-person main-character narration. The owner called figurative and cinematic lines stupid, and these would hide the message. I followed HJ10's own Hookz and Essential Story files instead: topic clarity in 1 to 2 seconds. Curiosity comes from literal questions ("Want more of them?", "Here's how to get more calls").
- **Used literal environment shifts.** HJ10's "shift 2 to 3 hyper-specific environments" and "micro-hook every 3 to 5 seconds" come only from real BlueFx example clips (machine shop, contractor, clinic, farm) and the real screen recording. No skits, no staged locations.
- **Used only the real ticking clock.** The only ticking clock is the real "about 5 minutes". No made-up deadlines and no "price goes up soon" (which the Pain Point and Skool templates use). The only scarcity is the real "one per business".
- **Left out "show the life".** The 13-step file's "paint what life looks like when leads flood in" and "status" points weren't used, because they need figurative language or bigger promises than the owner will make. "Show" is done with the real Ascent ad.
- **No humor, no clever closing line.** In Version 2 the "made with the same AI" line is on-screen text only and stated plainly. It's proof, not a joke.
- **No exclusion lists.** "Exclusion sharpens relevance" is done with a condition (paying customers and a website), never with "coaches, keep scrolling".
- **Kept familiar lines.** HJ10 says to rewrite anything predictable. I kept the owner's approved lines word for word, because they're clear and he already signed off on them.
- **Narrated as "we".** The narrator is a BlueFx presenter speaking as "we" to "you", not a first-person main character, and it never claims to be Szilard.
