# Video series: the 3 things your website needs to get you clients

Owner approved 2026-11-12. Three videos of 5 to 7 minutes each, on one page, unlocked by 3 emails two days apart. Every video ends with the same button: a free homepage mockup plus a free Google and ChatGPT check. The form asks only for their website, so they make the first move.

Idea notes: video-3-things-website.md. Spoken style: plain, short sentences, how Szilard talks. [Screen] lines are what's on screen; graphics come later.

---

## How these scripts are built (Jeff Walker style)
- Each part is about 800 spoken words, 5 to 6 minutes at a normal pace.
- Each part: a story or a real example first, then the teaching, then a small win they can do today, then a tease for the next part.
- Teach the what and the why fully. Keep the how light enough that most owners would rather have us do it.
- Every example is real (our own business, our clients, or a public example). Client names only where we already show them (Ascent). Others are described without names.
- [Screen] lines are what's on screen. Read the rest in your own words; it doesn't have to be word for word.

## Part 2: The video (about 6 min)

**Hook: the hundred-thousand-dollar roof**
"I recently heard a story on a podcast that stuck with me. A man named Ross sells metal roofs. Not cheap ones. Fifty thousand to two hundred thousand dollars per roof.
For years he sold them the old way. A salesman drives to the house, sits at the kitchen table for three hours, and wrestles the family until they sign.
Today he sells almost all of them online. The husband and wife watch a video at home, in their own time. They watch it again together. They sleep on it. Then they get on the phone for fifteen minutes, and the only thing left to talk about is the price.
Fifteen minutes, for a hundred-thousand-dollar roof. The video did the selling."

**Why video works**
"Think about how you decide who to hire. You want to know: do these people know what they're doing, and can I trust them?
Text can't really answer that. A photo of your logo can't answer that. But a video can, in about a minute. They see your face, they hear how you talk, they see your work, and they decide.
Ross said something else that's true for every business. When people watch a video, their guard comes down. There's no salesman in front of them, so they're not busy defending themselves. They just listen. And they can watch the important part again, or show it to their husband or wife.
We see the same thing every day. We make free video ads for business owners, and we can see when they watch. One owner watched his own video six times in about an hour, and bought from us the same night. Video moves people in a way text doesn't."

**The 3 parts of every video that works**
"Now, a video can also be boring and useless. Most business videos are. So here are the only three parts a good video needs.
One, the hook. The first five seconds decide whether they keep watching. So don't start with your logo spinning or 'Hi, we're ABC Plumbing, established 1998.' Start with their problem. 'Water under your sink at 10pm? Here's what to do.' Now they're listening.
Two, the offer. What you do, who it's for, and why you. Said clearly. Don't waste their time. Show your work while you talk.
Three, the call to action. Tell them exactly what to do next. Call this number. Book a time. Get your free quote. You'd be amazed how many videos just end, and the person watching has no idea what you want them to do."

**You on camera, or not**
"The best version is you, on camera. You don't need to be an actor. Just talk like you'd talk to a customer standing in front of you. People buy from people.
But I get it, a lot of owners don't want to be on camera. That's fine. Then at least have a 30-second to one-minute explainer video: what you do, why your offer is special, what to do next. Images of your work, a clear voice, done.
We actually built a tool that makes exactly this kind of video. It's called the AI Media Machine. It runs on these same three rules: hook, offer, call to action. You type in your website, and in a few minutes you have a finished video ad for your business. Or my team can make it for you, from your footage, your photos, or from nothing."

**Do this today**
"Here's your homework. Look at the top of your homepage. Is there a video? If yes, watch the first five seconds as if you were a stranger. Would you keep watching? And at the end, does it tell you what to do?
If there's no video, that's your biggest opportunity right there."

**Bridge to part 3**
"So now you have a website that's clear, and a video that gets people to call. There's just one problem left, and it's the biggest one. If nobody finds your website, none of this matters.
In part 3, I'll show you how people are finding businesses now, on Google and on ChatGPT, and four simple things that put you in front of them. One of them takes your web person about five minutes, and most websites still haven't done it."

**Call to action**
"Add your website below this video, and we'll send you your free homepage redesign and your Google and ChatGPT check. See you in part 3."

## Part 3: Getting found on Google and ChatGPT (about 7 min)

**Hook: back to the roofers**
"In part 1, I asked ChatGPT for the best roofer in Austin, and it named three companies out of hundreds. Today I'll show you why it picks the businesses it picks, and what you can do so it picks you."

**How people search now**
"For twenty years, getting found meant one thing: Google. You typed 'plumber near me,' you got a list, you clicked.
That's changing fast. Now a lot of people just open ChatGPT and ask: 'I need a plumber in Tampa who can come today, who's good?' And ChatGPT doesn't give them ten blue links. It gives them two or three names. That's it.
So your website now has two jobs. It has to show up on Google, and it has to be the kind of website ChatGPT trusts enough to recommend.
The good news: most of your competitors haven't figured this out yet. They're busy running their business. That's your window."

**Tip 1: let the AI in**
"First thing. Many websites, especially older WordPress sites, have a setting that blocks robots from reading the site. Years ago that made some sense, to keep out spam.
But when ChatGPT reads your website to learn about your business, it is a robot. So a lot of businesses are completely invisible to it, and have no idea.
Ask your web person to check this. It takes about five minutes. If you don't have a web person, my team can check it for you."

**Tip 2: a short summary on every page**
"Second. Every page on your website can have a short description hidden in its code. It's called the meta description. It's the little two-line summary you see under a link on Google.
For years most people left it empty or let it fill itself with junk. Now AI search reads it to understand what each page is about. If it's empty, you're making ChatGPT guess what you do. And it doesn't recommend businesses it has to guess about.
One or two plain sentences per page. What this page is, who it's for, where you are."

**Tip 3: answer the questions your customers ask**
"Third. Think about the questions customers ask you on the phone every week. How much does it cost? How long does it take? Do you come out to my area? Do you offer financing?
Put those questions on your website, with short, honest answers. Because those are exactly the questions people now type into ChatGPT. And it looks for the pages that answer them clearly."

**Tip 4: a video for each page, on YouTube**
"And the fourth one is my favorite, because most people completely miss it. Make a short video for each important page, and put it on YouTube.
Let me show you why.
[Screen: his YouTube analytics, traffic sources]
This is my own YouTube channel. We have about 22,000 subscribers. Look where a big part of the views come from. Google search. People type a question into Google, and Google shows them my video, right there in the results.
YouTube belongs to Google. Google loves showing videos. So when someone searches 'roofers near Austin,' your website can show up, and your video can show up on the same page. Two chances to be the one they call instead of one."

**Proof: Ascent Equipment**
"Let me show you what happens when you put these together. One of our clients, Dov, runs Ascent Equipment. They make machines for sign shops.
When we started, his website wasn't showing up in AI answers at all. Zero. We rebuilt the site, did exactly the things I just showed you, and in 14 days he was showing up in half of the AI answers for his main searches. The site loaded more than twice as fast, his website grade went from 71 to 93, and his traffic from search went up 45 percent.
Same business. Same products. The only thing that changed was the website."
[Screen: the Ascent numbers: grade 71 to 93, load 10.2s to 4.6s, AI answers 0% to 50%, +45% organic traffic]

**The machine**
"So let's put the whole series together.
A website that's clear, so people understand what you sell in five seconds. A video, so they trust you and call. And a website that Google and ChatGPT can read, with videos on YouTube, so people actually find you.
Now picture how many businesses in your area have all three. Very few. Most owners don't have the time, and it's not their job. They're busy doing the actual work.
