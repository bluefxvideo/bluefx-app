# Teaching video: the 3 things your website needs to get you clients

Owner idea, 2026-11-12. Recorded as he said it, lightly cleaned up. Not scripted yet; graphics come later.

Why: leads treat us as the one chasing them when we send unasked-for mockups. A teaching video makes us the expert, and the free offer at the end makes them ask us. Plain, spoken style, nothing complicated. Built around 3s because that's easy to remember.

## 1. The design: clear about what you sell
- The structure, in this order:
  1. a headline
  2. a great image
  3. bullet points explaining the offer
  4. what you've done so far, with clear pictures of your work
  5. testimonials
  6. content
- It has to work on mobile. About 80% of people visit on their phone.

## 2. Conversion: a video on the website
- Best: you on camera, explaining what you do, with examples and clear graphics.
- If you don't want to be on camera, at least a 30 to 60 second explainer video: what you do and why your offer is special. You can make it with the AI Media Machine.
- Every video needs 3 parts:
  1. a strong hook, so people stay and watch
  2. the offer, explained clearly without wasting their time
  3. a call to action: what they should do after watching
- The AI Media Machine's engine follows these 3 rules. Drop in your website and it makes the video.

## 3. Traffic: get found on Google and ChatGPT
A website nobody sees doesn't get you anything. More and more people now ask ChatGPT ("where can I find a plumber / roofing company near me?"), and it recommends a few businesses based on location and interest. You need to be one of them.
1. **Let the AI bots in.** Many websites, especially on WordPress, still block bots, and ChatGPT reading your site is a bot. Unblock it. Your web person can do this, or our team can.
2. **A short summary at the top of every page.** (He couldn't remember the term: the meta description, the short page summary that shows under the link in search.) It didn't matter much in the past. AI search now relies on it heavily.
3. **(He forgot this one while speaking.)** Possible fillers, for him to choose: an FAQ on each page that answers what customers ask, or the business details (name, address, phone, services, area) written the same way everywhere, or Google reviews.
4. **A short video for each page, uploaded to YouTube.** Google sees the page as important and often shows the video itself in results. Show his own YouTube stats: much of the traffic comes straight from Google search. So for "roofers near Austin" both your website and your YouTube video can show on the same results page.
- The point: most businesses in your industry don't have proper videos and a site prepared for search, because they're busy running the business. What if your marketing ran like a machine, getting you interested leads from the two places people search, Google and ChatGPT?
